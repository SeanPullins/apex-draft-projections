The simple version

APEX is a decision system, not one magic score.

Most draft boards collapse everything into a rank. APEX keeps the important questions separate so users can see what we believe, how sure we are, and what evidence could change the call.

Layer 1

Market prior

The consensus board is the starting point. APEX does not pretend the market is useless.

Layer 2

Current evidence

Live production and football evidence show where the player is running above or below the current prior.

Layer 3

Translation topology

APEX asks whether performance survives different roles and environments — pressure, depth, alignment, blocking context, and more.

Layer 4

Decision layer

Instead of forcing a new rank, APEX can say: hold, review up, review down, or acquire more evidence.

What the confidence colors mean

GREEN means the projection is comparatively stable across the validated uncertainty signals. AMBER means one important uncertainty dimension is elevated. RED means multiple uncertainty signals are elevated.

They are not talent grades. A top prospect can be RED because the decision is expensive and important evidence is missing.

What “What would change our mind?” means

For each covered prospect, APEX simulates the missing or low-confidence football context under a weak-evidence and strong-evidence scenario. The context that can move the projection most becomes the next scouting question.

That turns uncertainty into something actionable instead of hiding it inside one score.

Important: The 2027 Translation Topology snapshot uses private PFF college history through 2025. Live 2026 evidence is a separate current-season sensor. APEX shows that freshness limit instead of implying 2026 topology data exists when it does not.
